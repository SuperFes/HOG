# The globe: a voxel planet of maths and changes

Oct 31, 2026

The world is voxels on a sphere, modelled on Enshrouded's voxel world and stretched into a planet. A generator, written fresh, says what is at any point and at any resolution. A sparse store holds only what has changed: digging, building, water, slides, lava. What players see is a surface drawn from the voxels, so the world looks like a clean modern game, not blocks.

The research behind this is in `reports/Editable voxel planet architecture.md`, with notes in `research_notes/`. It replaces the hexel plan, which was tried first and has since been removed; its sparse tree and windows are in the history for when the store needs them. The old `hog::planet` generator and its heightfield are never used or read.

## Rules

- **Maths plus changes.** The generator is a pure, deterministic function of the planet's description and a point. The store holds only what differs from it. Nothing generated is saved.
- **Bit-identical everywhere.** The server and every client get the same bits from the generator, on every platform, checked in CI.
- **No stored heights.** A planet-scale reference level may exist only as physics computed inside the generator: crust, isostasy, basins. It is never saved, it never defines the surface on its own, and nothing outside the generator reads it.
- **Amounts, counted.** Voxels hold how much of each thing they contain. Water, rock, sand and heat are counted, and every step keeps the counts, in tests.
- **Many worlds.** A planet is a description made of physical knobs. Earth, Mars, Venus and the Moon are the first presets, and alien worlds are other settings.
- **Every landform has a home.** Each feature class gets a term in the generator, the store or the simulation, judged by stills of a fixed suite of spots, one per class.
- **Things fall apart when they ought.** Unlike Enshrouded, builds need support and wear out, so keeping a city standing is real, ongoing work for players, bots and NPCs. Building must not become a chore: sensible builds stand with room to spare, failure shows warning signs first, and repair is easy.
- **Beauty from life.** Enshrouded's beauty is placed by hand; ours grows. Plants spread and die back, animals graze and wear trails, and people build, farm and neglect, all by simulated rules, so the world is shaped by what lives in it rather than by a designer.
- **Libraries only when excellent.** We use one only if it's clearly excellent and has an MIT-compatible licence. Otherwise we build it ourselves.

## Layout

The planet is a cube inflated into a sphere. Each of the six faces is a square grid of columns, warped onto its sixth of the sphere by the tangent mapping, so a cell's area varies by at most 1.414 from face centre to corner. Every column points at the planet's centre, so down is always straight down a column. Across the cube's 12 edges grids meet at an angle, and at its 8 corners three cells meet instead of four. One neighbour function handles both and is tested at every edge and corner.

A cell is addressed by level, face and `(i, j)`. A level has `6 · 4^level` cells, and each splits into exactly four at the next level, so levels nest and sums carry exactly.

**A shell, not a ball.** Voxels run from an impassable floor a few kilometres down up to a sky ceiling, both preset knobs. Below the floor and above the ceiling there are no voxels, only calculation: core, mantle, gravity, heat flow, the air above. Magma enters through the floor as a computed source. Over a few kilometres of depth, cells barely change size.

## The generator

`sample(point, lod)` returns the distance to the nearest solid surface, the material makeup, any loose amounts and the temperature. It is built from the planet's knobs:

- **Planet-scale fields,** computed on demand per column and never saved: crustal provinces, the reference level from isostasy, climate, and dichotomies like Mars's.
- **3D terms:** strata, eroded relief, karst and caves in depth bands, arches and overhangs, craters by size and age, volcanoes and magma bodies, dunes, glacial forms, soils and regolith.
- **One coarse drainage solve per seed:** stream-power erosion to steady state over the whole planet, because a river depends on everything upstream. It sets where rivers, lakes and deltas lie. Detail is added on top.

Terms blend by smooth union and subtraction, weighted by physical fields, not by hand.

Each material counts only through its properties: density, melting point, strength and heat capacity, and for water its porosity, hydraulic conductivity, the slopes a loose heap of it holds dry and soaked, the drag a flow needs to carry it and how fast it goes once carried. Under the sea the ground is soaked to its pores; above, the generator leaves it dry for rain to wet and the air to dry.

What v1 builds, all from the knobs:
- **Reference level:** a province field split at the high crust's share, the provinces floating apart by the isostatic step across margins as wide as that step's slope allows. The radius stands at the sea, filling its share, or at the mean ground on dry worlds. The shell's floor lies `floor_depth` under the lowest ground surveyed.
- **Relief:** mountain belts on long sinuous lines, set by the regime; peaks within them; rolling hills everywhere; ridges down plate worlds' ocean floors. Where water runs, gullies are cut from coarse to fine. Each width runs down the ground coarser than itself, so valleys branch as drainage does, without a simulation.
- **Craters:** counts from Neukum's lunar chronology by age and impact flux, capped at equilibrium. Thick air filters out the small ones. Bowls sit below the gravity-set transition, flat floors and central peaks above it, and creep wears each crater by its age.
- **Strata and soils:** sedimentary beds where a volatile has run, folded where mountains rose and stripped from them; soil, mud, beach sand and snow where water cycles; regolith that thickens with age where it doesn't. The loose cover creeps: rock is generated rough, and the cover's surface is the rock's smoothed over the creep length (16 m, the detail soils take their slope at) and lifted by the depth the cover is made at, so it fills the hollows under that surface and leaves the knobs above it bare. Steeper ground keeps less of it, from about 27° to none by 45°, and colluvium is what has pooled deeper than the soil, not a depth by slope. Every depth shapes the ground, so each grades smoothly: the shore from soil to mud, the beach in by height and slope, snow by temperature. A crater's crest is rounded as far as creep has worn it, and never sharper than the detail asked for, as a kink in the slope would otherwise step the cover. On Earth the cover cuts the ground's relief under 32 m to about a quarter of the rock's. Coarser detail than 16 m lays the cover on its own ground as made, which stands where the fine ground does on average to a centimetre.
- **Caves:** passages along limestone beds and chambers in them, and lava tubes under basalt flows, carved out of the rock. Each keeps to the bed or basement it formed in, as the shape both share, so its walls run on smoothly across the beds' faces. A chamber needs about as much rock over it as the widest span a bed of its rock holds as a beam, about 60 m on Earth and more under weaker gravity; under thinner rock its roof has fallen in and it shrinks away.

A column's terms depend on direction alone, so `Generator::column` computes them once and samples any radius along it. The ground's slope, which the distance needs, is measured by central differences, four extra columns each. Voxels are filled through `Generator::columns`, for any cells of one level, which takes the slope from neighbouring cells instead. Stills come from `hog-stills`: a map, a relief square, a section and a plan slice, all drawn by sampling the 3D field.

## The store

The store is one sparse tree per face over `(i, j, shell)`. Empty means "ask the generator", a uniform block of edits is a single tile, and edited blocks are palette-coded 8³ bricks. Matter comes in two kinds:
- **Shaped matter,** such as rock, earth and buildings, carries a distance, its makeup and its condition: how worn, cracked or rotten it is.
- **Loose matter,** such as water, sand, rubble, snow and lava, carries amounts. A solid voxel carries its pore water as a liquid amount too, up to its material's porosity.

Only the server writes the store.

What v1 holds:
- **Voxels** stand on the cells of the level whose width is nearest the layers' thickness: level 18 at the default 115 km and 0.5 m, so cells are 0.53–0.75 m wide. A voxel's value is taken at its middle. It holds fixed-point values: distance in millimetres, the surface's normal folded onto an octahedron in two bytes (to a degree), condition, a grain and a liquid with amounts of the voxel's volume, and temperature in eighths of a kelvin. Distances and normals are kept within a band of four layers either side of a surface; beyond it, only the side counts. The generator gives each sample its normal: the ground's from its tilt, a cave wall's from differences of the cave's distance. Digging and building write their shape's.
- **Bricks** of 8³ are the unit stored, saved and sent. A brick that is one voxel throughout is held as that voxel. Otherwise each quantity is packed apart, as one value, a palette of 1–16-bit indices, or in full. So a brick the surface passes through keeps its distances in full and a few bits a voxel for the rest.
- **The store** is a hash of changed bricks keyed by face, `i`, `j` and layer. A brick changed anywhere is held whole. One written back as generated is let go. Tiles bigger than a brick, and the tree over them, wait until uniform edits that large appear.
- **Filling:** a brick is filled through `Generator::columns`, where each column takes the ground's slope from the cells beside it. That is 2.2–2.4× cheaper than each measuring its own, and as true against the real distance (about 5% either way). A cell's column comes out the same in any square, so unchanged voxels are the same everywhere.
- **Saving** writes only the bricks changed since the last save, as rows of the world's SQLite file under a header of the layers they stand on. Loading refuses bricks saved on other layers.
- **Edits** so far are balls dug and built. All the generated ground is shaped matter; sand and snow turn loose when the simulation disturbs them.

## The simulation

The simulation runs on the server, only in chunks near players or events:
- **Water and lava** move between the sections of open voxels up each column and the four columns beside it, by the difference in their heads, the same every way: the columns are square and so is the flow. Liquid pushed into a full section presses in beyond full and the head that gives pushes on, so it finds its level through a tunnel. Shaped matter is the only blocker, so a dam, a sluice, a dug channel or a built aqueduct all work by being built or dug; nothing is a special case. A current drags on its bed by its depth and the slope of its surface, and where the drag exceeds what the bed withstands it wears it away: grain piled there first, then the shaped ground, whose surface it pushes in millimetre by millimetre until the voxel comes open. What it wears off travels with the liquid as a load and settles out at the material's speed as grain on the bed below, the voxel's own count of loose matter, which the client draws as it likes: grain takes room from the liquid and is ground to the flow once it fills its voxel. Loose matter in motion is counted in voxels; only the shaped surface is a distance.

The flow is a game system that stands in for many things, so it is designed against the list of them rather than any one. Each is a source, a sink, a blocker, a reading of the state or a rule at a contact, and nothing else; a new use must fit one of those or the model is wrong:
- *Shaping:* diverting a river with a dug channel, damming a valley, draining a marsh or a mine, flooding a quarry, a canal, a well into the groundwater, a moat.
- *Sources and sinks:* the sea beyond the edge at its level, with tides later; rain and snowmelt; springs and artesian heads where pressed-in liquid finds a way out; evaporation; pumps and drains built by players.
- *Readings:* the surface level and the current in any column for boats, swimmers, wheels and mills; wetness of the ground for crops and for plants and animals that need standing water; the flux across a face for erosion and for carrying soil, sand and silt. The flow leaves a moisture record as it runs: the pore water it soaks into the ground, and per column how long and how deep water stood or ran there, kept as a map that vegetation reads to grow thick by water and thin away from it.
- *Contacts:* water meeting lava makes rock and steam; lava cools and stiffens; water freezes over and thaws; soil soaked through softens and gives way; a flooded build wears.
- *Reach:* flow in the open, through caves, through the pores of soil and sandstone, and in built pipes alike, so that every one of these is the same code with a different voxel under it.
- **Rain and drying** act on the voxels' own liquid, so wetness travels with the bricks and nothing else is sent. Every game minute the server takes the weather over each player (rain where under half of what falls is snow; dryness from the air's humidity) and, for the 49 × 49 columns about them, `flow::Wetting` lets rain soak into the ground's top voxel as fast as its conductivity allows, lies any more on it as a film for the flow to run off (waking that brick), and takes water back out, film first, at up to 0.3 mm an hour in warm dry air, scaled by warmth (cold ground and standing water are left alone). Bricks the flow holds are changed there, the rest through the world. A voxel is a metre deep and rain soaks only centimetres, so `globe::dampness` reads a voxel's water as lying in its top 5 cm: a few millimetres dampen the surface, a few centimetres soak it. The mesh's wet darkening and the grip rule both read that.
- **Sand and rubble** slide when steeper than they hold, in true distances. What a heap holds is one rule over one description of the ground, `globe::Surface`: the shaped bed at the top of a column, the grain lying on it and how wet it is. A thick layer is held by its own friction (repose, less when soaked), a thin one by its grip on the bed, and cohesion holds a dusting of snow or clay where dry sand would run, so a drift lets go where a dusting clings. The character reads the same rule for the slope it stands on without sliding (`grip_at`), and the server's move check does too: a rise steeper than that slope holds is refused once a jump's worth of allowance (1.6 m, regained at 1.2 m a second) is spent. Every floor of a column slumps, caves' included, and grain with open space under it falls. Any drop is a link: grain over a short drop slumps across at once; over a cliff (more than two voxels) it leaves as **grain in flight**.
- **Grain in flight** is the region's, not the voxels': a lump with a position, velocity, material and amount, moved a step at a time under radial gravity, so it takes the time a fall takes. On the ground it rebounds by its material's `bounce`, loses speed to the surface's friction, rolls on while the slope is steeper than the surface holds, and when it stops it is piled into the column under it, which slumps on if it is too steep. A lump carries on for 30 s at most, and at most 2048 are in flight at once, past which grain slumps across drops as before. The server tells each player the lumps within 300 m (`Grains`, unreliable, nearest 256, every tick while any fly and once more when none do); the client carries each on under gravity between messages and draws it. The bricks sent when it lands are how everyone hears it has. Flight is not saved: grain in the air is lost with a brick let go, and no brick is let go while any flies.
- **Collapse** checks connectivity in a bounded region, plus a load rule that lets arches stand. It's partial and local: what's overloaded sags and cracks first, then the failed part falls as rubble.
- **Wear** lowers condition by each material's own process: weather, water, rot, rust, frost and load. Worn parts get weaker, so neglect ends in collapse. Wear is what makes maintenance jobs: repair, drainage, clearing rubble, roads.
- **Lava** stiffens and freezes as it cools.

Updates are integer, ordered by a hash and repeatable. Clients receive changed bricks within their interest radius and interpolate between updates.

## The client

The client meshes changed chunks on worker threads:
- **Shaped matter** is meshed by dual contouring, so crisp and smooth forms come out of one surface.
- **Loose matter** is meshed by surface nets.

Detail falls off with distance without cracks, and materials blend by triplanar texturing. Positions are 64-bit on the server and camera-relative floats on the GPU, with reversed-Z depth.

What the mesher draws:
- **Chunks** of 32 columns square, each meshed from its own voxels and those of the boxes beside it, taken over the cube's edges from the next face. Each edge between voxels is drawn by one chunk: the one holding its lower end, or across the cube's edges, the lower face. A box's corners are taken in an order set by the world's voxels, so chunks either side find its vertex to the last bit. At the cube's 8 corners, where three columns stand round a box, its vertex is placed in the world's terms, so the corners close too.
- **Detail by distance:** a chunk at detail n is a cell 2ⁿ times as wide, still 32 columns across, drawn from every 2ⁿ-th voxel along each axis and generated as much coarser. Each level's voxels are among the next finer's, so levels nest; over the cube's edges each face keeps its own lattice, and the rows there are unevenly wide. Distances are told in 2ⁿ millimetres so the widened band fits. A quadtree on each face keeps every chunk a set number of its widths from the eye (3 by default), then splits until neighbours are at most one detail apart, and across the cube's edges at the same one.
- **Seams:** chunks are meshed by dual contouring over a tiling of boxes, each chunk's lattice standing on a grid as fine as its finest neighbour. Where details differ, a polygon joins three or four boxes of both sizes round each shortest edge, and a coarser box's vertex takes in the crossings on the finer boxes' edges on its faces, as octree dual contouring does. Each voxel is generated at the finest detail of the boxes it's a corner of, so every chunk finds it alike. Where nothing is drawn beside a chunk, past the horizon, it's boxed as if drawn at the chunk's own detail; across a cube's edge from a coarser chunk, at that chunk's, as its boxes straddle the edge at their own size. A chunk's mesh depends on how those beside it are drawn, and is made again when that changes.
- **Two surfaces:** ground against open space, and liquid against air. Ground is shaped matter, by its distance, and grain piled on it, as high as it fills its voxel, whichever is nearer; a voxel's material says which side it's on, as a distance under half a millimetre rounds to none. Liquid stands up each run of open voxels at the fill of the highest holding any, and the run and the ground under it take that level, so a film shallower than half a voxel still shows; liquid within a voxel of the sea's level is the sea, drawn at its level exactly. The liquid's surface runs on two columns under the shore, so the ground, not a voxel's step, draws the waterline.
- **Vertices** are placed by dual contouring: where the planes tangent to the surface at its crossings of a cube's edges meet, nearest the crossings' mean along directions the planes leave free. Each crossing takes the normal of the voxel nearer it, so a crease keeps both its sides; a dug hollow's lip comes within 9 cm, where surface nets round it by 16. Shading takes the crossings' normals too. The liquid holds no normals and uses its field's gradient. Crossings still come from straight lines between voxels, which miss a crease's true place by a few centimetres where an edge's ends belong to different surfaces.
- **Detail:** voxels are generated with nothing finer than two of them across, as finer detail would alias into steps a voxel apart. Coarser detail is the fine ground filtered, not resampled: the terms dropped are zero-mean, a voxel's material is what lies at its top, so the first under the ground shows the cover however thin, soils take the slope at 16 m whatever the detail, and the sea is drawn at its level over any seabed below it, however much shallower than a voxel. A quadtree chunk once split stays split until the eye is a quarter again further off, so the ground doesn't flicker between details.
- **Where:** from 16 m under a chunk's lowest ground to over its highest, around the sea's level apart from that, round every cave in sight, and through held changes next to any of those. Caves are found by casting: one ray down the middle column of each square of four by four columns, stepping by a bound on the distance to the nearest cave that can never overshoot (the noise's steepest slope for its slope at the point, the rock's openness a reach higher, every bed within reach counted), so a stretch of the cast where a cave may lie within the square becomes a band to draw. Caves close with depth where the stress round a passage, two and a half times the rock's weight over it, overcomes a fifth of its strength, so deeper under weaker gravity and never a constant. What lies under the ground is seen only from inside it: chunks within 96 m of the eye are meshed with caves between 64 m under and over the band of radii the eye is in, 32 m tall, and again when the eye leaves it; physics keeps its ground the same way. Elsewhere only the ground's own band is drawn.
- **The client:** `--globe`, or `--globe-planet` with a preset or a planet file, meshes the quadtree's chunks out to the horizon, and no less than a kilometre round the eye, on the job pool, nearest first. Each chunk's mesh is uploaded once and drawn apart: its ground as blocks, and its liquid standing clear of the sea's level as still water the sea draws after itself, with the sea's own optics, reflections and refraction and the waves the wind grows on still water; liquid at the sea's level is the sea's own, which the sea draws over the ground. A chunk no longer wanted stays until those in its place are shown, so the ground doesn't open. Flight keeps above the generated ground.
- **Materials:** each material has a tile painted from its look: a colour it averages to, patches of an accent, mottling and grain, as the old ground's biomes had. A vertex names its material's tile; a triangle whose corners name different tiles gets corners of its own naming all three, so the tiles blend across it by the corners' weights, and the rest share vertices as before. Tiles are projected along the planet's axes by the normal, at 2 m and 16 m, the far one breaking the near one's repetition, each straying from the tile's mean so the two together still average to it. Each vertex carries how wet the ground is (`globe::dampness`), packed beside its corner in a byte; wet ground is drawn darker, to near half when soaked, so the seabed, a shore the water has seeped into and a pile the water stands in all show it. Not yet: strata banding on cliffs from the generator's bed depth, and roughness.
- **Server and streaming (stage 9):** the server owns the voxel world: the generator at the planet's seed and size, over the bricks changed, kept with the world's file and saved with it. Only the server writes it. A player asks to dig out or build up a ball (`X` and `V` in the client, 1.5 m across 3 m ahead); the server applies it and tells everyone the bricks changed, in bulk messages of at most forty, and a player joining every brick held. A client holds the bricks it's sent in place of the generator's and meshes again the chunks they touch, having first waited for the chunks in flight, as the mesher reads the store without locks. Unchanged ground is never sent: the generator is bit-identical everywhere, so each side generates what it needs; the server only where it edits or simulates. Alone, without a server, the client edits its own world the same way. Players walk on the globe: the physics ground is its finest chunks meshed round each player, on the server for move checks and in the client, let go where a brick changes and meshed again. The server builds nothing of the old planet any more: its landform, change log, weather, wildlife, forest and the debug probe over them are gone, and the world's id comes from the globe's knobs, so older saves start over. The client followed (2026-11-01): it draws the globe alone, always, the old ground, grass, trees, bushes, forest, rivers, shore and the woodlands feed gone with the `--globe`, `--blocks`, `--grass` and `--graphics` flags; its sky, sea, clouds, rain and glare passes stay and are fed by the globe. The sea is drawn by the ocean pass over the ground, so chunk meshes leave the liquid's surface out. What's kept to come back on the globe: the grass shaders and lattice, the material tiles. The libraries followed: the old generator, its change log, the weather, wildlife and forest simulations and the woodlands message are gone, and the planet's config keeps only its seed, size, ocean share, day, tilt and system. Not yet: who may dig where, edits by the simulation, bricks by distance rather than to everyone, and a planet preset told to clients.
- **Cost:** 42–86 ms a full-detail chunk on one thread, Venus to the Moon, and 220–350 ms over caves on Earth; coarser ones 40–80 ms, as the generator's work per column barely falls with coarser detail. A cave chunk's cost is its flagged depth, about 2 ms a metre, so what's in sight is all that's paid for: a load standing in a cave district takes 49 s of one thread, 25 s from 400 m up. Most of the rest is the generator's columns: each also takes the ground's slope at 16 m for its soils, nearly another column's work. About 470 chunks are drawn from the ground; from a kilometre up, about 560.

## Weather and the sea

Seasons and weather emerge from the computation; nothing is scripted.
- **The air** (`libs/weather`): layers over the cube-sphere columns (tangent mapping), about 1 km thick at 24 layers. Ground is smoothed to one cell width, and a layer is blocked if its middle is below the column's ground. Wind comes from a pressure projection with least-squares gradients fitted to each cell's real links, so skewed cells near cube edges and corners don't stretch it. Heat, humidity, cloud, rain and snow are carried by it. The server's `WeatherKeeper` advances it asynchronously (2 min game-time steps, 48 h spin-up) and saves it as the "AIRS" snapshot section.
- **Cloud heights** are read from the layers' cloud between their edges, so base, top and fog top vary smoothly and are led by buoyancy: cold cloud sinks, warm cloud rises, and terrain shapes it only through the flow.
- **To the client:** `protocol::CellWeather` per cell of `sim::CellGrid` (S2-style, quadratic mapping, not the air's mapping). The client's `Forecast` builds per-face cube maps (cover, rain, snow, humidity, heights, drift). A clear cell keeps its neighbours' typical heights, and heights follow the drifted cloud, so cloud edges don't slope to the ground. `cloud_march.slang` marches the maps.
- **The sea:** planet-fixed plane-wave trains (16 bands × 40 headings) summed on the CPU (`hog::waves::surface_at`) and the GPU (`waves.slang`), kept equal by a test. Depth shoals them, limits their height and breaks them. A breaker's face is drawn back and its lip dropped by a parametric curl (`breaker.cpp`), weighted by each wave's share of the summed amplitude so waves in step curl together, and only where the Iribarren number says the break plunges. Real voxel waves (FLIP) were tried and set aside: too costly, no clear plunge. The server gives each sea cell a wind sea from its wind (a fully grown stub).
- **Not yet:** the curl has not been seen on screen or timed on the 860M, and has no spray or foam; wind sea has no fetch, duration, swell or current; heat transport, land wetness and snow/ice are stubs; the lowest air runs warm and dry; layers are too coarse for valley pooling; cube-edge stretching and a few seam cells are unexplained.

## Build order

1. **Cells:** cube-sphere addresses, the tangent mapping and its inverse, exact neighbours across edges and corners, cell areas and volumes, levels; the sparse tree and windows ported from diamonds to faces.
2. **The planet's description:** physical knobs, derived values and the four presets.
3. **Generator v1:** reference level, strata, eroded relief, caves, craters, soils; golden hashes on every platform.
4. **Store:** face trees, tiles, bricks, both kinds of matter, saving and loading.
5. **Mesher v1:** surface nets; the client's `--globe` draws the new world, and the hexel code is removed.
6. **Mesher v2:** dual contouring.
7. **Detail by distance,** crack-free across chunks and faces.
8. **Materials and texturing.**
9. **Server authority and streaming.**
10. **Water and soil:** the loose cover crept smooth in the generator (done); the materials' porosity, conductivity, repose dry and soaked, critical shear and erodibility, and the ground soaked to its pores under the sea (done); the flow between column sections over square cells, which the server runs where the world is touched and lets sleep once still (done); the current's drag wearing its bed and carrying what it wears off as grain that settles out again by its speed (done); the client's inland water, grain and wet ground (done); the settle test: the simulation touched on generated seabed, shore, lowland and mountain moves nothing, wears nothing, writes nothing and sleeps (done). Lakes and rivers planet-wide wait for the drainage solve.
11. **Sand and rubble:** slumping to what they hold (the rule over linked columns, and the region's grain on the top ground slumping each step, are done; friction, cohesion and wetness per material; rock grain holds 40°; sub-voxel ground comes from the shaped distance; the character's footing; grain falling, bouncing and rolling in flight, told to players, are done; floors inside caves under a roof and grain left hanging when the ground under it is dug (it falls as lumps) and grain crossing the cube's edges onto the next face are done; fallen rock lies below steep ground, stamped into the generator's distance field as ellipsoids up to 4.5 m, so it meshes, bears weight and digs like any ground; blocks too small for a detail drop out of it; boulders as bodies that roll when disturbed, and temporary imprints, are not).
12. **Collapse and wear.**
13. **Lava and magma.**
14. **The drainage solve, rivers, lakes and coasts.**
15. **Life:** plants, animals and people shaping the ground they live on.

Each stage ends in tests and stills. The report's build order says how each one is judged.
